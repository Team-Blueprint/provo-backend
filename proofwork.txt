One-line description
Proofwork turns every payment an informal worker receives into a confirmed job record that lenders
can trust.
Problem
Emem is a bricklayer in Uyo. He works for different clients and moves between building sites. Some
clients pay by bank transfer and others pay cash. At the end of the month the money is there, but
nothing shows how he earned it. He wants a loan to buy his own tools so he can take on more jobs. A
lender looking at his bank statement sees only deposits. It cannot tell a client payment from a gift or a
loan repayment, so it has no reliable proof of steady income.
Emem is not an exception.
• About 83% of jobs in Africa were informal in 2024 (ILO and UN Economic Commission for Africa).
• In Nigeria, payment access rose from 22% to 52% of adults between 2010 and 2023, while credit
access grew by only about 4 points (EFInA, Access to Financial Services in Nigeria, 2023).
Payments are everywhere, but credit is not following.
• Among informal businesses that got credit, 70.7% got it from friends and family and 12.2% from
banks (Moniepoint informal economy report, 2024). That survey covers businesses, not individual
workers, and Moniepoint is a lender.
No source found so far measures how many workers are refused credit for lack of proof of income.
This is an inference to be tested through the interviews in the implementation plan.
Solution
Each worker gets a dedicated Paga account number and a payment link. When a client pays through
either, the client confirms the payment was for work and picks a job type from a list. Each confirmed
job becomes a line in the worker's record, with a receipt issued by Proofwork. Over months the record
shows confirmed jobs, repeat clients and active months. The worker shares a report with a lender,
with client identities tokenized.
One rule governs the product: a record is created by the client's payment and confirmation, never by
the worker's own entry. A job the worker types in himself is a draft that counts for nothing. This is
what makes the record safe for a lender to rely on, and it is why Proofwork issues receipts only for
work it can verify.
Proofwork is an income verification product for lending. It is not a job platform. It does not find work
for the worker. It makes the income from jobs he already has count toward credit.
Target audience
Primary users are informal workers who have named clients and are paid per job. Scope stays broad
for now, with niching later. Three example groups for the pitch:
1 Building trades: bricklayers, painters, electricians, plumbers and carpenters. Payments are
sizeable, clients hire again, and tools are an obvious reason to borrow. Cash payment on site is
the weak spot.
2 Tailors, fashion makers and hairdressers: repeat clients, transfers are common, and equipment
such as a sewing machine or a generator is a typical loan purpose. Payments are smaller, so a
useful record takes more jobs.
3 Mechanics and auto repairers: a named customer pays per repair, owners return, and the
equipment need is clear. Cash is common here too.
Market traders and drivers who serve walk-in customers are a poor fit. They receive many small
anonymous payments, so payer confirmation does not work well for them.
Two other parties use the product: clients, who pay and confirm, and lenders such as microfinance
banks and fintech lenders, who read the report.
Workflow
Worker side
1 The worker signs up over WhatsApp or USSD and gets a dedicated Paga account number and a
payment link.
2 After a job he sends the link to the client, or gives the account number.
3 He gets a message when money arrives, showing whether the job is confirmed or waiting for
confirmation.
4 For a cash job he logs it by message. It stays a draft until the client confirms.
5 He can ask for his record at any time and share a lender report link.
Client side
1 The client opens the link, picks a job type, enters the amount and their phone number, and pays
through Paga.
2 For a direct transfer to the account number, the client gets a message asking whether the
payment was for work by the worker, and picks a job type.
3 For cash, the client gets the same message after the worker logs the job.
4 A client without WhatsApp gets an SMS link, or can use a USSD code.
5 The client gets a receipt for each confirmed job.
Lender side
1 The lender opens the worker's report: confirmed jobs, repeat clients, active months and a score.
2 Clients appear as tokens, for example "Client A3F, phone verified, 4 payments over 3 months",
with first name and last initial. Tokens come from a hash of the payer's phone number, so repeat
clients stay visible without exposing identity.
3 Full identities are released only for a dispute, with the client's consent.
Grades, strongest first
1 Paid through the link, client phone verified.
2 Transfer to the dedicated account, confirmed by the client.
3 Cash, confirmed by the client. This carries a capped share of the score and is never the basis for
a loan on its own.

Technology
• Paga Collect API: persistent payment accounts (dedicated virtual accounts that can be funded
from any bank or mobile money operator, with automatic deposit notifications) and payment
requests with callbacks, which can accept bank transfer and USSD funding.
• A webhook service that receives each payment event and creates a record only when a client
payment arrives.
• A confirmation flow over WhatsApp and SMS, with USSD for basic phones. WhatsApp business
messages need pre-approved templates, and a USSD shortcode needs a telco aggregator and
takes weeks, so USSD is mocked for the demo.
• A scoring layer: count unique confirmed payers instead of payments, weight repeat clients, flag
payments that circle between the same few accounts, give less weight to payers with no other
history in the system, and cap the share of cash jobs.
• A lender report view with tokenized payers.
• A lending partner. Paga's developer page lists Collect, Persistent Payment Account, Business
REST and Merchant Services APIs, and none is lending. Loans need a licensed lender, with Paga
used for repayment collection.
Why Paga: it offers the collection and notification rails above, Paga Engine pitches fraud detection
and compliance tooling, and Doroki already runs pay-from-any-bank flows for small merchants. No
public Paga product builds an income record for informal workers, so Proofwork is a layer on top of
Paga rails.
What makes it different
Lenders such as Moniepoint already underwrite from transaction histories, but a lender only sees
payments that pass through its own rails. Emem's income comes from many banks and in cash.
Proofwork differs in four ways:
• It is lender-neutral, so any lender can read the record.
• It includes cash and cross-bank payments.
• Payers confirm each payment, so the record does not rest on the worker's word.
• Each job is a structured entry with a job type and a receipt, not a bare deposit.
This is an argument, not a sourced finding, and it needs testing with lenders. Job marketplaces such
as Wrkman, LaborHack and Handymesh are a different category, because they find work and
Proofwork records it.

4 Write the fraud rules on one slide.
5 Shape the pitch: the one-line description, Emem's story, the data, the demo, the difference from
existing lenders, and a next-steps slide.

Questions and answers
Why would a client confirm a payment? It takes one tap with no app, and they get a receipt
naming the job. The client gets little else, so the design asks almost nothing of them. Cash
confirmation rates will be lower, which is why cash grades lower.
Why not a transfer with a description and a bank receipt? The description is free text, a
screenshot can be edited, and nothing links payments into a history a lender can read.
How does the worker find Proofwork? His reason to join is the loan. The strongest route is a lender
who asks applicants without statements to bring a Proofwork record. Other routes are trade
associations and his own clients, who see Proofwork each time they pay.
Does Proofwork help him find jobs? No. It records the income from jobs he already has. A record
needs several months of confirmed jobs from distinct payers, so a worker with few clients builds it
slowly. Job matching is a later addition.
What stops fake jobs? Only a client's payment and confirmation create a record. A friend could still
pay and confirm a job that never happened, so the score rewards many distinct payers over several
months, flags circular flows and gives new payers less weight. This makes fraud costly and visible
without ruling it out. Loan officers can still verify by calling clients.
Why is client identity tokenized? A lender needs to know payers are distinct, real people, not who
they are. Tokens keep repeat clients visible, and full names stay with Proofwork.
What about the law? Nigeria's data protection act requires consent that is freely given, specific,
informed and clear, and sets data minimisation and purpose limitation as principles. The CBN
consumer protection framework requires written consent before personal data is shared with a third
party. The consent design should be checked with someone qualified before launch.

Who lends? Proofwork acts as a report provider, not a lender, which keeps regulatory exposure low.
A microfinance bank or fintech lender would be the pilot partner. No lender is confirmed.
Who pays? Proposed: free for the worker, with the lender paying per verified report. This is
unvalidated.
